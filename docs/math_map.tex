\begin{abstract}
This note writes out every map from token input to token output in
\texttt{gemma-2-2b}, states what is linear and what is not, and derives the
reachability certificate in both the form we have been using (a probe halfspace at an
intermediate layer) and the form the last meeting proposed (an argmax cone in token
space). The two are the \emph{same formula} with a different readout vector, which is
the point: switching to the token-space version costs no new machinery and removes
the failure mode that produced our negative result. Section~\ref{sec:diag} gives the
diagnosis that follows, with measured numbers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Notation and the forward map}

Let $d = 2304$ be the model width, $L = 26$ the number of decoder blocks, and
$V = 256{,}000$ the vocabulary. Write $E \in \R^{V \times d}$ for the token embedding
matrix, which in this model is \emph{tied}: the same matrix serves as the unembedding.
Let $\gamma \in \R^d$ be the gain of the final RMSNorm.

Given a prompt $x_{1:T}$, the residual stream is
\begin{align}
  \vh^{(0)}_t &= \sqrt{d}\, E[x_t], \label{eq:embed}\\
  \vh^{(l+1)} &= \vh^{(l)} + \mathrm{Attn}_l\!\left(\mathrm{RN}(\vh^{(l)})\right)
                  + \mathrm{MLP}_l\!\left(\mathrm{RN}(\cdot)\right),
                  \qquad l = 0,\dots,L-1, \label{eq:block}
\end{align}
where each block mixes across positions, so $\vh^{(l+1)}_t$ depends on
$\vh^{(l)}_{1:t}$. Then the final norm and the unembedding:
\begin{align}
  \vz_t &= \mathrm{RN}_\gamma\!\left(\vh^{(L)}_t\right)
        = \frac{\vh^{(L)}_t}{\mathrm{rms}(\vh^{(L)}_t)} \odot (\bm{1} + \gamma),
        \qquad \mathrm{rms}(\vh) = \frac{\norm{\vh}}{\sqrt{d}}, \label{eq:rmsnorm}\\
  \bm{u}_t &= E\,\vz_t \in \R^{V}, \label{eq:logits}\\
  \tilde{\bm{u}}_t &= c \tanh(\bm{u}_t / c), \qquad c = 30 \ \ (\text{final logit
    softcapping}), \label{eq:softcap}\\
  p_t &= \mathrm{softmax}(\tilde{\bm{u}}_t / T). \label{eq:softmax}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Convention warning (a real bug we hit).}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rmsnorm} folds the gain into $\vz$, so the matrix that pairs with
$\vz$ in~\eqref{eq:logits} is the \emph{plain} $E$. The other valid convention writes
$\vz' = \vh^{(L)}/\mathrm{rms}(\vh^{(L)})$ and $W = E \odot (\bm{1}+\gamma)$, giving
$\bm{u} = W \vz'$. Both produce identical logits and identical argmax. They do
\emph{not} produce identical \emph{distances}, and every budget in this note is a
distance. Mixing them inflates every reported $\varepsilon$ by a factor that varies
per coordinate. Fix the convention once and state it.

\section{Three facts that make the output layer tractable}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Softcapping is argmax-irrelevant.} $s \mapsto c\tanh(s/c)$ is strictly
increasing and applied coordinatewise, so it preserves the ordering of
$\bm{u}_t$. Every statement about \emph{which token wins} may be made about the raw
$\bm{u}_t = E\vz_t$. Statements about \emph{probabilities} may not.

\item \textbf{At $T = 0$ the decoder is exactly an argmax}, so the map from $\vz$ to
the emitted token is piecewise constant, with pieces
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}_j \;=\; \left\{ \vz \in \R^d \;:\;
    (E[j] - E[k]) \cdot \vz \ge 0 \ \ \forall k \ne j \right\},
  \label{eq:cone}
\end{equation}
a convex polyhedral cone with $V - 1 = 255{,}999$ faces. This is the object the
meeting asked for: \emph{the target set, defined in token space}. It is not a
learned probe, not a heuristic, and not something a readout can dissociate from,
because membership in $\mathcal{C}_j$ \emph{is} the behavior.

\item \textbf{RMSNorm is degree-zero homogeneous}, i.e.
$\mathrm{RN}_\gamma(c\,\vh) = \mathrm{RN}_\gamma(\vh)$ for $c > 0$. Differentiating
\eqref{eq:rmsnorm} gives the Jacobian at the final norm,
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{pre}} \;=\; \frac{\partial \vz}{\partial \vh^{(L)}}
  \;=\; \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\norm{\vh^{(L)}}}\;
        \mathrm{diag}(\bm{1}+\gamma)\, P_\perp,
  \qquad P_\perp = I - \hat{\vh}\hat{\vh}^{\!\top},
  \label{eq:rmsjac}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{\vh} = \vh^{(L)}/\norm{\vh^{(L)}}$. Two consequences, both measurable:
$P_\perp$ \emph{annihilates the radial component} of any steering vector added before
the norm, and the surviving part is scaled by $\sqrt{d}/\norm{\vh^{(L)}}$, which for
our activations is $48/726 \approx 0.066$, a sixteenfold contraction partly undone by
the gain.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Temperature.} For $T > 0$ the piecewise-constant picture softens into
$p_j \propto \exp(\tilde u_j / T)$. Two regimes matter. As $T \to 0^+$ we recover
\eqref{eq:cone} exactly. For $T > 0$, $\log p_j - \log p_k = (\tilde u_j - \tilde
u_k)/T$, so the \emph{logit margin} $M$ defined below converts directly into a
log-odds shift of $M/T$: a perturbation that consumes a fraction $\phi$ of the margin
multiplies the odds of the target against the incumbent by $e^{\phi M / T}$. This is
why the margin, not the activation norm, is the currency. Note also that at $T=0$
determinism is only as good as the kernels: batch-invariance failure in RMSNorm,
matmul and attention makes ``temperature 0'' non-reproducible under \emph{dynamic
batching}, so bit-exactness claims require a fixed batch size or batch-invariant ops.

\section{The certificate, stated once, instantiated twice}

\subsection{General form}

Fix an injection \emph{site} $S$ (a layer index, or the input/output of the final
norm) and a readout: a vector $\vw$ and a threshold $t$ such that the property of
interest is $\vw \cdot \vz \ge t$, where $\vz$ is the activation the readout lives on.
Let $A_S$ be the Jacobian of the map from the site to $\vz$, evaluated at the
unperturbed activation. A perturbation $\vd$ injected at $S$ moves the readout by
$\vw \cdot A_S \vd = (A_S^{\!\top}\vw)\cdot\vd$ to first order. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  g = t - \vw\cdot\vz \quad(\text{gap}), \qquad
  m = \norm{A_S^{\!\top}\vw} \quad(\text{controllability margin}), \qquad
  \varepsilon^\star = \frac{g}{m}. \;}
  \label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
$\varepsilon^\star$ is the least $\norm{\vd}$ that reaches the halfspace, and it is a
point-to-hyperplane distance: $m$ is the \emph{gain} of the site on this readout, and
$g$ is the \emph{distance to travel}. The minimiser is
$\vd^\star = \varepsilon^\star\, A_S^{\!\top}\vw / m$.

Two structural remarks. First, $m$ carries all the dynamics; nothing else about the
network enters. Second, for any \emph{other} unit direction $\vu$ at the same site,
the budget required along $\vu$ is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon(\vu) \;=\; \frac{g}{\lvert (A_S^{\!\top}\vw)\cdot \vu \rvert}
  \;=\; \frac{\varepsilon^\star}{\alpha}, \qquad
  \alpha \;=\; \frac{\lvert (A_S^{\!\top}\vw)\cdot\vu\rvert}{\norm{A_S^{\!\top}\vw}}
  \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in [0,1]$ is the cosine between the chosen actuator and the optimal
one. \textbf{Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha} is the whole diagnosis.} A steering experiment
that fixes a budget without reporting $\alpha$ cannot tell ``the property is not
causally actuable'' from ``we pushed almost perpendicular to the thing that moves it.''

\subsection{Instantiation 1: the probe halfspace (what we ran)}

Site $S$ = input of decoder layer $l_{\mathrm{src}}$; readout at layer
$l_{\mathrm{tgt}}$; $\vw$ = the contrastive mean-difference direction
$\mathrm{unit}\!\left(\bar{\vh}^{\mathrm{tgt}}_{y=1} -
\bar{\vh}^{\mathrm{tgt}}_{y=0}\right)$; and $t = t_{02}$, obtained by fitting a
one-dimensional logistic regression of the label on the score $\vw\cdot\vh^{(\mathrm{tgt})}$
and solving for the score at which $P(y{=}1) = 0.2$:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(a\,s + b) = 0.2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  t_{02} = \frac{\log(0.2/0.8) - b}{a} = \frac{-1.3863 - b}{a}.
  \label{eq:t02}
\end{equation}
$A_S = J$, the layer-to-layer Jacobian, obtained by one vector--Jacobian product
(never $d$ forward passes), so $m = \norm{J^{\!\top}\vw}$.

\textbf{The failure mode is structural.} $\vw$ is fitted, so $\vw\cdot\vz \ge t$ is a
statement about a \emph{readout}, and nothing forces a readout to be the thing the
rest of the network consumes. We measured exactly that: readout displacement tracked
the certificate to $R^2 = 0.999$ while behavior did not move. This gap is not new,
and we should stop presenting it as the finding. Elazar et al.\ (amnesic probing,
TACL 2021) warned against inferring behavior from probes; LEACE (Belrose et al.\ 2023,
\S5.3) exhibits a decodability peak at a different layer from the causal peak; AxBench
(Wu et al.\ 2025), on \emph{this model}, reports that better classification does not
yield better steering. Our contribution has to be the certificate, not the phenomenon.

\subsection{Instantiation 2: the argmax cone (what to run)}

Now take the readout to be a face of \eqref{eq:cone}. Let $j_{\mathrm{top}}$ be the
current argmax at the position that chooses the next word, and $j_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ the
token we want instead. Put
\begin{equation}
  \va \;=\; E[j_{\mathrm{tgt}}] - E[j_{\mathrm{top}}], \qquad
  M \;=\; -\,\va\cdot\vz \;=\; u_{j_{\mathrm{top}}} - u_{j_{\mathrm{tgt}}} \;>\; 0 .
  \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
Then ``the model emits $j_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ rather than $j_{\mathrm{top}}$'' is exactly
$\va\cdot\vz \ge 0$, and \eqref{eq:cert} applies verbatim with
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\vw := \va, \qquad t := 0, \qquad g = M, \qquad
  m = \norm{A_S^{\!\top}\va}, \qquad
  \varepsilon^\star_{\text{token}} = \frac{M}{\norm{A_S^{\!\top}\va}} .\;}
  \label{eq:tokencert}
\end{equation}
\emph{Same formula, same code, same single VJP.} What changes is that $\va$ is not
fitted: it is read off the unembedding, and $\va\cdot\vz \ge 0$ is not a proxy for the
behavior but a restatement of it. The readout-versus-behavior dissociation becomes
impossible by construction.

\paragraph{From one face to the whole cone.} \eqref{eq:tokencert} certifies crossing
\emph{one} face. To make $j_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ the true argmax we need all of
\eqref{eq:cone}, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  \vd^\star = \arg\min \norm{\vd} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
  \left(A_S^{\!\top}(E[j_{\mathrm{tgt}}]-E[k])\right)\cdot \vd \;\ge\;
  (E[k]-E[j_{\mathrm{tgt}}])\cdot\vz \quad \forall k .
  \label{eq:coneqp}
\end{equation}
Only the constraint \emph{normals} depend on the site; the offsets are logit gaps
either way. With $V$ constraints this looks impossible, but the active set is tiny:
solve the dual on the faces seen so far, ask the full vocabulary who wins, add the
violator, repeat. Measured on \texttt{gemma-2-2b}, the active set has $1$ to $3$
faces and $\norm{\vd^\star}$ exceeds the single-face bound
$M/\norm{A_S^{\!\top}\va}$ by about $8.8\%$, so the single face is a good but not
exact proxy and the full solve is worth doing. The dual of \eqref{eq:coneqp} is
$\min_{\lambda \ge 0} \tfrac12 \lambda^{\!\top} G \lambda - b^{\!\top}\lambda$ with
$G = \mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{\!\top}$ and $\vd = \mathcal{A}^{\!\top}\lambda$, which is
a few microseconds. Every solution is then \emph{verified} by an explicit
full-vocabulary argmax check, so a reported certificate is a proof rather than an
optimizer's opinion.

\paragraph{Non-emptiness.} $\mathcal{C}_j$ can be empty. By Demeter et al.\ (ACL 2020)
a token is emittable for \emph{some} $\vz$ if and only if its unembedding row is a
vertex of the convex hull of all rows; by minimax,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\norm{\vz}\le 1}\ \min_{k\ne j}\ (E[j]-E[k])\cdot\vz
  \;=\; \min_{\lambda\in\Delta}\ \norm{\textstyle\sum_k \lambda_k (E[j]-E[k])}
  \;=\; \operatorname{dist}\!\left(E[j], \mathrm{conv}\{E[k]\}_{k\ne j}\right),
  \label{eq:hull}
\end{equation}
so a positive value is proved by exhibiting a witness $\vz$ and a zero value by
exhibiting the convex weights. We ran this (Frank--Wolfe, batched): all $182$ tested
tokens, including all $102$ country first-tokens and the $16$ lowest-norm rows in the
vocabulary, are achievable. So the target sets are non-empty, and $d = 2304$ is well
above the regime where unargmaxability bites (Grivas et al.\ 2022). \emph{This clears
a necessary condition only}: it says the cone is non-empty, not that it is reachable
from where the model actually sits under a bounded perturbation. That is
\eqref{eq:tokencert}.

\paragraph{The harness assertion.} At the post-norm site with $\vd = \vd^\star$ from
\eqref{eq:coneqp}, the token flip is a \emph{theorem}, not a hypothesis: we verified
$\arg\max_k E[k]\cdot(\vz+\vd^\star) = j_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ in closed form. If the running
model does not emit $j_{\mathrm{tgt}}$, the injection code is wrong. Every steering
run should carry this oracle arm. We have never had one.

\subsection{The broadcast operator}\label{sec:broadcast}

The site table above writes $A_S$ as though a perturbation were a single vector in $\R^d$. It is
not. Every certificate we have computed added $\vd$ at \emph{every} position of the
prompt. That is a different linear map and it deserves its own symbol. Let
$B:\R^d \to \R^{T\times d}$ be the broadcast $B\vd = \bm 1_T \otimes \vd$. Then the map
being inverted is $A_S \circ B$, and because $B$ is a sum of coordinate injections its
adjoint sums the per-position gradients:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:broadcast}
  (A_S B)^{\!\top} \va \;=\; B^{\!\top} A_S^{\!\top}\va
  \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\partial\, (\va\cdot\vz)}{\partial \vh^{(l)}_t}\,,
  \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
  \frac{\partial\,(\va\cdot\vz)}{\partial \vh^{(l)}_{\mathrm{last}}}
\end{equation}
for the single-position convention. Equation~\eqref{eq:broadcast} is
\texttt{g.sum(dim=1)} in \texttt{src/token\_jac.py:82} and
\texttt{src/reach\_jlens.py:97}; the right-hand object is \texttt{m\_last}. Their ratio
is the broadcast gain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bgain}
  \rho(l) \;=\; \mathrm{median}_i\;
  \frac{\bigl\lVert \sum_t \partial r_i/\partial \vh^{(l)}_t \bigr\rVert}
       {\bigl\lVert \partial r_i/\partial \vh^{(l)}_{\mathrm{last}} \bigr\rVert},
\end{equation}
measured at $1.355$ for \texttt{cities} layer $16$ and $2.016$ for
\texttt{common\_claim} layer $8$. Two consequences. The broadcast site has the larger
margin, so a budget quoted under broadcast is easier to meet than the same number
quoted per position, and $m_{\mathrm{last}}$ is the honest per-position figure. And
ActAdd-style single-position injection instantiates the right-hand map, not the
left-hand one, so comparing our $\varepsilon$ to a published ActAdd $\varepsilon$
without dividing through by $\rho$ compares two different operators. The energy remark
elsewhere in this note ($\varepsilon\sqrt T$) is a consequence of $B$, not a substitute
for writing it.

\subsection{The cross-layer convention behind the \texttt{\_asis} cosines}
\label{sec:asis}

$\alpha$ in \eqref{eq:alpha} is a cosine, so both arguments must live in the same
space. For the directions labelled \texttt{\_asis} they do not, and the label is there
to say so. Those directions are read verbatim from
\texttt{reach\_dirs\_*.npz}, where they were fitted at the \emph{target layer}
$\vh^{(l_{\mathrm{tgt}})}$; the $\alpha$ we report takes their cosine against $\va$ in
\emph{post-norm} coordinates $\vz$, with no transport between the two
(\texttt{src/token\_geom.py:395--400}). So
$\alpha_{\texttt{mean\_diff\_tgt\_asis}} = 0.01325$ is the cosine between $\va$ and the
raw coordinate vector we actually injected. That is the operationally correct quantity,
because it answers what the direction we really steered with bought us, and it is
\emph{not} a coordinate-free statement about the concept. The basis-correct version
would transport the target-layer direction forward,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
  \vu^{\mathrm{tr}} \;=\; \frac{J_{l_{\mathrm{tgt}}\to\vz}\,\vu}
                                {\norm{J_{l_{\mathrm{tgt}}\to\vz}\,\vu}},
  \qquad
  \alpha^{\mathrm{tr}}(\vu) = \frac{\lvert \va\cdot\vu^{\mathrm{tr}}\rvert}{\norm{\va}},
\end{equation}
which costs one JVP per statement and has not been computed. Until it is, every
\texttt{\_asis} number carries this paragraph.

\section{When is a norm budget the right currency?}\label{sec:gain}

Everything above prices a perturbation by its Euclidean norm $\varepsilon$. That is a
modelling choice, and the meeting challenged it directly: \emph{small perturbations in
different directions can have vastly different chains}. Stated precisely, define for a
site $S$, a unit direction $\vu$, and a budget $\varepsilon$ the \emph{realized gain}
and the \emph{realized slope}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
  G_S(\vu,\varepsilon) \;=\; \frac{\norm{\vz(\varepsilon\vu) - \vz(\bm 0)}}{\varepsilon},
  \qquad
  \sigma_S(\vu,\varepsilon) \;=\; \frac{\va\cdot\bigl(\vz(\varepsilon\vu)-\vz(\bm
  0)\bigr)}{\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
where $\vz(\cdot)$ is the post-final-norm activation the perturbed forward pass
actually produces. These are measured, not predicted: no Jacobian appears in
\eqref{eq:gain}.

Two limits pin down what the objection can mean, and they are not the same claim.

\paragraph{Anisotropy.} Fix $\varepsilon$ and vary $\vu$. If
$G_S$ is constant in $\vu$ the site is isotropic and $\varepsilon$ is a fair price. The
statistic is the spread $G^{p90}/G^{p10}$ over directions. Note that anisotropy alone is
\emph{not} an obstruction: it is exactly what $\alpha$ already measures at the post-norm
site, and $\varepsilon(\vu) = \varepsilon^\star/\alpha$ is precisely the rescaling that
repairs it. An anisotropic-but-linear site needs a change of units, not a change of
theory.

\paragraph{Nonlinearity.} Fix $\vu$ and vary $\varepsilon$. For a linear
$A_S$, $G_S(\vu,\varepsilon) = \norm{A_S\vu}$ with \emph{no dependence on
$\varepsilon$ whatsoever}. So
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledep}
  \kappa_S(\vu) \;=\; \frac{G_S(\vu,\varepsilon_{\max})}{G_S(\vu,\varepsilon_{\min})}
\end{equation}
is a pure nonlinearity reading, and $\kappa_S \equiv 1$ is the exact statement that the
first-order certificate is valid over the whole budget range. This is the claim that
would actually threaten the program: if $\kappa$ is far from $1$ and varies with $\vu$,
then no fixed budget, rescaled or not, certifies anything, and the certificate must be
restated in terms of a bound on the realized gain rather than on $\varepsilon$.

At the post-norm site $A_S = I$, so \eqref{eq:gain} collapses to $G = 1$ and $\sigma =
\va\cdot\vu$ identically, for every direction and every budget. That is arithmetic, so
it serves as a second harness assertion of the same kind as the oracle arm: a post-norm
run that does not reproduce it is measuring something other than what it claims.

Read plainly: the token flip is \emph{cheap} ($3.4\%$ of $\norm{\vz}$), and every
candidate actuator is \emph{orthogonal} to the direction that buys it. The comparison
that matters is not $\alpha$ against $1$ but $\alpha$ against the alignment a
\emph{random} direction would achieve, which in $d = 2304$ is
$\mathbb{E}\lvert\hat{\va}\cdot\vu\rvert = \sqrt{2/\pi d} = 0.0166$. All three
directions sit at or below that floor (Wilcoxon against chance, $p < 10^{-6}$ in every
case). They are not weakly aligned with the decision; they are \emph{uninformative}
about it, and the fitted final-layer direction is four times \emph{worse} than chance.

By \eqref{eq:alpha} the budget we spent therefore bought on the order of one percent
of a flip. A behavioral null at that budget is not evidence that truth is un-actuable;
it is evidence that we measured the budget in the wrong units. The unit to report is
the fraction of the margin consumed,
\begin{equation}
  \phi \;=\; \frac{M - M_{\text{after}}}{M}
  \;=\; \frac{\varepsilon\,\lvert (A_S^{\!\top}\va)\cdot\vu\rvert}{M},
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and $\phi$ has never appeared in any of our results tables.

\paragraph{A counter-explanation that the data rejects.} Tan et al.\ (2024) report that
in many datasets nearly half of examples are \emph{anti-steerable}, which would make an
aggregate flat while individual statements move in both directions. Our per-statement
arm already swept signed scales, so this is testable without a GPU. On \texttt{cities},
of $199$ statements, $103$ are byte-identical at every scale, $68$ change text without
changing verdict, and $28$ change verdict, splitting $24$ with the direction against
$4$ against it (exact two-sided sign test $p = 1.8\times 10^{-4}$). The effect is
small, directionally \emph{correct}, and highly significant; cancellation is not the
mechanism, inertness is, which is what \eqref{eq:alpha} predicts. On
\texttt{common\_claim} the picture differs: only $8$ statements are inert, $56$ move,
and $21$ of those move the wrong way ($p = 0.08$), which is the anti-steerability
regime Tan et al.\ describe. The two datasets fail for two different reasons and must
be reported separately.

\paragraph{Two methodological defects in the existing runs, both now fixed in code.}

ActAdd adds at one aligned position; the injected energy is then $\varepsilon\sqrt{T}$
rather than $\varepsilon$, and the attention sink is perturbed. Second,
